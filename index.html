Our Challenge

Our Challenge

The FBI estimates around 500,000 predators are online every day. Using the internet without taking precautions can lead to exposure to harmful content. It can lead to revealing private information, which usually leads to scamming, getting hacked, and identity theft.

It can also lead to cyberbullying — around 4% of people who get cyberbullied commit suicide within the first 6 months of reporting it to someone. Knowing that 33% of young people have been victims of cyberbullying, this works out to roughly a 1.33% chance of suicide caused by online bullying alone.

If this topic is close to home: the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988 in the US) is free and confidential, any time.
Why Is It an Issue?

Why Is It an Issue?

If parents don't trust their kids with electronics, when they move on to adulthood they won't fully understand how to use the internet safely. It then creates a cycle: parents distrust → kids hide → parents distrust more.

It is a problem because it creates distrust between parents and children. Children may hide their online activity instead of asking for help, which can make them less safe online. It also causes kids to push their parents away and become distant in daily life.

5 Whys

5 Whys

Why are teens exposed to online risks?

Teens are exposed to online risks because they often face situations online without getting guidance from a trusted person beforehand.

Why don't they ask a trusted adult for help?

They don't ask a trusted adult for help because many worry they'll be judged, punished, or have their devices taken away.

Why do parents respond with punishment or monitoring?

Parents respond with punishment or monitoring because they feel responsible for keeping their children safe, but don't fully understand today's online world.

Why don't parents understand today's online world?

Parents don't understand today's online world because technology and online culture have evolved faster than they've been able to learn and adapt.

Why has this created a long-lasting issue?

This has created a long-lasting issue because parents and teens have developed different levels of digital understanding, which creates a gap in communication and trust. This gap leads parents to rely on surveillance instead of teaching digital skills, while teens become less likely to seek support when they need it the most.

Current Solutions

Current Solutions

What parents rely on

  • Screen time limits
  • Monitoring apps
  • Taking devices away
  • Blocking websites
  • Location tracking
  • Age restrictions
  • Passwords / 2FA
  • Content filters

The results

  • Less trust
  • Secret accounts, secret devices, etc.
  • Kids learn to hide things
  • Issues aren't discussed until it's too late
  • Low engagement
  • Negative feelings due to restrictions
Current solutions focus on controlling teens instead of helping families build trust and digital skills.
Solution Principles

Solution Principles

We want a way for parents to trust their kids and allow them to make independent choices and decisions while still understanding the risks and dangers of the internet; this includes knowing when to talk to a parent or a guardian when you don't feel safe or properly secure online, and knowing how to steer clear from cyberbullies, and much more.

An Overview of What We Want Our Solution to Achieve

An Overview of What We Want Our Solution to Achieve

For our solution, as a team we decided on creating a toolkit: teens will show adults what they know about online safety, like how to use social media responsibly and how to play games and use social media while being responsible and safe.

In exchange, adults can help them stay safe online by showing them what they know: not to talk to people you don't know, not to share personal info, etc. There will be some parent guides and tips where there will be a 1-on-1 explaining the dangers of the internet and how to avoid them. They could have a progress system to see if teens are acting correctly and showing more signs of responsibility and safety.

Focus Group

Focus Group

A focus group is a discussion with a group of people led by a moderator. For this, parents could meet together to talk about their teenagers' online safety, discuss challenges they have faced, share what has improved, and suggest ways to help teens stay safe online. This allows researchers to collect detailed feedback and different perspectives.

Who

Parents of teenagers, teenagers, and a moderator.

What

A group discussion where parents share their experiences and opinions about their teenagers' social media usage and online safety.

When

At different stages of the program, such as after workshops or 1-on-1 sessions, to review progress and gather feedback on the solution.

Where

At a school, meeting room, or online on a video call.

Why

To collect feedback, understand parents' and teens' experiences, identify problems, and find ways to improve online safety for teenagers.

Answer: Yes, our solution encourages teenagers and parents to work together when making decisions about online safety instead of relying only on rules or supervision. The focus groups give both parties the opportunity to share their experiences, learn from each other, and provide feedback that improves the toolkit. This aligns with the deliverable by ensuring that young people have a voice in decision making, leading to more effective and meaningful solutions for digital life.

The Toolkit

The Toolkit

1

Meet up with the child you wish to have this session with.

2

Tell them that this is not a punishment and they are free to leave whenever they want.

3

Ask them questions.

  • Do you always feel safe when you're online?
  • How do you protect yourself when you're online?
  • Have you ever heard of someone your age getting cyberbullied?
  • What would you do if you got cyberbullied?
  • Has a stranger ever messaged you first? What did you do?
  • Is there anything online that's ever made you feel uncomfortable?
  • Do you know what to do if someone asks for personal info, like your address or school?
  • Who would you go to if something online ever felt wrong?
4

Track their progress with the tracker on the next page.

5

Do this twice a week and come back in 7 days and see if they have made more progress.
